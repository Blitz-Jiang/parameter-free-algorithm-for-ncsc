\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Related work}
We review complexity results for regularized Newton and trust-region
methods, emphasizing adaptation to unknown smoothness parameters and
the distinction between first- and second-order stationarity.
In the comparisons below, $\Delta=f(x_0)-\inf f$, and $\epsilon$ and
$\delta$ denote the gradient and negative-curvature tolerances,
respectively.

\paragraph{Regularized Newton methods and classical complexity.}
\citet{nesterov2006cubic} establish the classical cubic-regularization
benchmark
$O\!\left(1+\Delta[\sqrt{L_0}\epsilon^{-3/2}+L_0^2\delta^{-3}]\right)$
for second-order stationarity when the Hessian Lipschitz constant
$L_0$ is known.
\citet{cartis2011adaptive2} analyze adaptive cubic regularization
(ARC), retaining the $O(\epsilon^{-3/2})$ first-order evaluation bound
while allowing approximate Hessians and inexact subproblem solutions
under suitable accuracy conditions.
\citet{cartis2012second} derive second-order evaluation bounds for
both cubic regularization and classical trust-region methods,
including sharp $\delta^{-3}$ dependence for their algorithmic
frameworks.
\citet{grapiglia2017regularized} develop regularized Newton methods
that adapt to unknown H\"older regularity of the Hessian and attain
the corresponding nonconvex first-order complexity rates.
\citet{cartis2019universal} extend this universality principle to
higher-order Taylor models, with bounds that reflect the regularity,
regularization power, and model accuracy without requiring the
smoothness parameters in advance.

\paragraph{Trust-region methods and parameter adaptivity.}
\citet{curtis2017trust} introduce TRACE, whose modified acceptance
and radius-update rules attain $O(\epsilon^{-3/2})$ first-order
evaluation complexity under Lipschitz-Hessian smoothness.
\citet{curtis2018concise} give concise analyses of radius-controlled
trust-region methods, including a fixed-radius scheme attaining
$O(1+\Delta\sqrt{L_0}\epsilon^{-3/2})$ first-order complexity when $L_0$ and
$\epsilon$ are available to choose the radius.
\citet{curtis2021trust} develop exact and Newton--CG trust-region
variants with strong second-order iteration and Hessian-vector
product complexity guarantees, explicitly accounting for inexact
subproblem solutions.
\citet{jiang2026beyond} combine gradient-dependent quadratic
regularization with a trust-region constraint in the universal
trust-region (UTR) method, obtaining
$\widetilde O(\epsilon^{-3/2})$ nonconvex second-order complexity
under Lipschitz-Hessian smoothness, together with convex and local
convergence guarantees and an adaptive implementation.
Their nonconvex analysis incurs a multiplicative logarithmic factor,
and their regularized quadratic subproblem is the algorithmic
starting point of the present work.

\citet{hamad2022consistently} introduce the consistently adaptive
trust-region method (CAT), which achieves the sharp first-order
leading bound $O(\Delta\sqrt{L_0}\epsilon^{-3/2})$, up to additive
logarithmic terms, without knowing $L_0$ or $\Delta$.
\citet{hamad2024simple} refine CAT into a practical method with
inexact subproblem solves, retaining this leading bound while
improving its implementation and numerical performance.
\citet{sirotenko2026on} extend the CAT approach to unknown
$\nu$-H\"older Hessian regularity, obtaining
$O(\epsilon^{-(2+\nu)/(1+\nu)})$ nonconvex first-order complexity
for exact UniCAT, as well as universal convex and local rates.
These first-order results motivate sharp parameter dependence, but
do not provide the independent curvature-tolerance guarantee studied
here.

\paragraph{Generalized smoothness.}
\citet{zhang2020clipping} motivate gradient-dependent smoothness
through a Hessian bound growing with the gradient norm and use it
to explain the advantage of clipped and normalized gradient methods.
\citet{xie2024trust} extend trust-region analysis to stochastic
problems with generalized first- and second-order smoothness,
obtaining $O(\epsilon^{-7/2})$ stochastic oracle complexity for
approximate second-order stationarity and improving it to
$O(\epsilon^{-3})$ through variance reduction.
\citet{diouane2026generalized} establish parameter-agnostic
first-order complexity for classical trust-region methods under
generalized gradient smoothness, allowing general Hessian
approximations, including limited-memory quasi-Newton models.
\citet{gratton2026fast} analyze an adaptive Newton method with
negative-curvature steps under a local two-point generalized
Hessian condition and a lower Hessian bound on the initial sublevel
set, obtaining second-order complexity of order
$|\log\epsilon|(\epsilon^{-3/2}+\delta^{-3})$ when problem
parameters are fixed.

\citet{semenov2026gradient} introduce gradient-normalized smoothness
to relate Hessian approximation quality to convergence of
gradient-regularized methods, with adaptive search removing the need
to specify the local smoothness scale.
Under the global two-point condition
\[
 \norm{\nabla^2f(y)-\nabla^2f(x)}
 \le (L_0+L_1\norm{\nabla f(x)})\norm{y-x}
 \qquad\text{for all }x,y,
\]
their exact-Hessian case gives the first-order leading
bound $O\!\left(\Delta[\sqrt{L_0}\epsilon^{-3/2}
+\sqrt{L_1}\epsilon^{-1}]\right)$.
This specialization requires the exact Hessian to be an admissible
positive-semidefinite model; their general nonconvex theory also
accounts for Hessian approximation error and targets first-order
stationarity.

Most directly related is \citet{fang2026parameter}, who analyze a
parameter-free cubic-regularized Newton method under
$f\in C^3$ and the pointwise condition
$\norm{\nabla^3 f(x)}\le L_0+L_1\norm{\nabla f(x)}$.
Their Corollary~1 gives the second-order oracle bound
\[
 O\!\left(\Delta\left[
 \frac{\sqrt{L_0}}{\epsilon^{3/2}}+
 \frac{\sqrt{L_1}}{\epsilon}+
 \frac{L_0^2}{\delta^3}+
 \frac{L_1}{\delta}\right]\right)+\widetilde O(1),
\]
where the additive logarithmic terms are independent of
$\epsilon$ and $\delta$, and inexact cubic subproblem solutions are
allowed.
Their pointwise condition is weaker than the corresponding global
two-point Hessian condition within the common $C^3$ class; the latter
condition, however, can also be imposed on merely $C^2$ functions.
We work under the same pointwise assumption and obtain the same
four-term rate using quadratic trust-region subproblems.
The distinction is the UTR construction and its analysis: a joint
scale--gradient potential controls boundary and interior steps and
removes the multiplicative logarithmic loss of the earlier UTR
analysis.

\paragraph{Lower bounds.}
\citet{carmon2020lower} prove the oracle lower bound
$\Omega(\Delta\sqrt{L_0}\epsilon^{-3/2})$ for first-order
stationarity on the Lipschitz-Hessian class, even for randomized
algorithms with access to all derivatives.
\citet{arjevani2020second} establish a complementary
negative-curvature lower bound whose noiseless second-order
specialization is $\Omega(\Delta L_0^2\delta^{-3})$ for
zero-respecting algorithms.
These results provide the sharp Lipschitz-Hessian benchmarks for
the two tolerance terms; optimality of the additional
$L_1$-dependent terms under generalized smoothness remains open.

\paragraph{Notations.}
Write $[z]_+:=\max\{z,0\}$ and
$\lp t:=\max\{\log t,0\}$ for $t>0$, with $\lp0:=0$.
The algorithm below uses quadratic trust-region subproblems and
observable acceptance tests, without knowledge of $L_0$ or $L_1$.
Let $\Delta:=f(x_0)-f_*$. Vector norms are Euclidean and tensor
norms are induced operator norms.
